Every claim checked against the official voting booklet, with the line that decides it.

GeaNaVid reads the Swiss Federal Council's voting booklet and a claim in German, French or Italian. It answers Gea, Na or Vid, Romansh for yes, no and empty: the booklet supports the claim, contradicts it, or does not say. Every answer points to the page and passage it rests on.

The problem

Voters hear the claim. The answer is on page 4 of 40.

Before every federal vote, each household receives the booklet: the proposal, the legal text, the arguments of the committee and the position of the Federal Council and Parliament, in German, French and Italian. During the campaign, claims about it travel fast. Checking one means finding the right paragraph in 40 to 90 pages, often in another language than the claim.

Claim · German

„Gemäss dem Abstimmungstext ist die Bevölkerung seit Einführung der Personenfreizügigkeit im Jahr 2002 um rund 500'000 Personen gewachsen.“

Booklet of 14 June 2026 · page 4 · In Kürze

„Seit Einführung der Personenfreizügigkeit im Jahr 2002 ist die Bevölkerung um rund 1,7 Millionen Personen gewachsen.“

Na · contradiction (2)

Approach

Start with a page. Climb only when unsure.

Sending the whole booklet to the model costs 10,000 to 45,000 tokens per claim. GeaNaVid gives Apertus a handful of passages instead and widens the context only when the verdict is weak.

  1. Read the booklet

    The PDF is split into passages with page, proposal, section and author, including the note that a double page was written by the committee. No language model, no tokens, 0.3 s for 40 pages.

  2. Find the passages

    BM25 picks the five best passages and their neighbours. A German claim against a French booklet is searched in the German version of the same booklet and mapped back by page layout.

  3. Ask Apertus

    One call to Apertus v1.5 with numbered passages. The answer is strict JSON: first the passages that decide, then the label. Evidence is always real booklet text with page and offsets.

  4. Escalate when unsure

    A verdict without evidence or with low probability gets one more look with the surrounding pages. Most claims stop after the first step.

Status · 1 October 2026

First measurements

Official demo of the OST dataset and its cross-lingual variants: 74 claim–booklet pairs. Macro-F1 over entailment, neutral and contradiction; input tokens per claim from the API.

Context given to Apertus 8B 70B Tokens per claim
GeaNaVid ladder (passages, escalation) 0.92 0.94 ≈ 2,100
Retrieved passages only 0.91 0.96 ≈ 1,700
Whole booklet (baseline) 0.81 0.98 ≈ 18,200
Claim without booklet (control) 0.09 0.09 ≈ 330
  • Less context helps the small model: 8B is better with 9 % of the booklet than with all of it.
  • 70B barely needs the whole booklet: 0.96 against 0.98 at a tenth of the tokens.
  • Cross-lingual retrieval without tokens: searching the parallel booklet lifts recall@5 from 0.74 to 0.95.
  • Long booklets break the baseline: on held-out claims for four longer booklets (40,000 tokens on average), the whole booklet drops to 0.60 (8B) and 0.85 (70B). The ladder keeps 0.88 and 0.94 with about 1,800 tokens.

Small samples: 23 distinct official claims (95 % intervals about ±0.07) and 221 held-out synthetic pairs whose labels still await a human check.
